When AI writes a large share of the code, the work of an engineering team moves. Producing code stops being the scarce part. Reviewing it, understanding it and deciding what the agent should prefer become the job.

That shift shows up in places leaders do not always look. Review queues grow while output charts look healthy. Old, accepted risks in the codebase get more expensive because AI makes them easier to find. Release dates slip because bugs are found faster than they can be fixed. And an agent makes stack and library choices at machine speed, often from defaults nobody in the company chose.

These posts are written for CTOs and engineering managers who are past the question of whether to adopt coding agents and are now dealing with what happens after.
