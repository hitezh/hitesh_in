Most companies now have AI activity and no one senior who owns it. Teams run pilots that never reach production, vendors pitch platforms nobody can evaluate, the bill grows, and the board keeps asking what the plan is.

A full-time Head of AI is the obvious answer and often the wrong first step. The role is expensive, slow to hire, and hard to scope before you know what AI should be doing for the business. A fractional Head of AI gives you the judgment now, for a few days a month, and makes the full-time hire easier when its time comes.

## What I take responsibility for

- **A short list of AI bets worth making.** Which use cases have a business case, which are theatre, and in what order to try them.
- **Build, buy or wait.** Vendor and model selection with the trade-offs written down, including what it will cost at production volume.
- **Pilots that tell the truth.** Each one designed with a baseline and a named owner, so the result is evidence and not a demo.
- **Governance your teams can live with.** What agents and coding tools may touch, who approves what, and how you would know afterwards what happened.
- **The AI bill.** Who owns it, what drives it, and where a smaller model does the job.
- **Bringing the leadership team along.** Regular reviews with the CEO and the board in plain language, and [training for the leadership team](/ai-training/) where it is needed.

## How it works

1. We start with an [advisory hour](/work-with-me/) so you can judge the fit before committing to anything.
2. In the first month I review what is already running, talk to the people doing the work, and come back with priorities and the first pilot.
3. From then on I work with your teams for an agreed number of days each month, on site in Hyderabad or remotely, and report to whoever owns the outcome.
4. When you are ready for a full-time AI leader, I help you define the role, interview for it and hand over.
